Cinderella

Threshold signing for Nostr, with rules and a veto.

Your Nostr key is split into shares. No single machine can sign alone. Every share checks what it is asked to sign before it helps, and the most dangerous changes wait for you first.

Tested end to end in a dry run (throwaway keys, October 2026). Not yet audited. Not yet holding a real key.

The problem

One nsec does everything. If it leaks, the attacker can rewrite your profile, delete your notes and become you.

FROSTR already splits a key so two of three shares must agree. But a share that agrees to anything is only a speed bump.

Cinderella makes every share look before it signs.

How it works

  • Tiers per kind

    Normal notes and reactions go through; identity-changing events are held.

  • Rate limits

    A sudden burst of signing is refused.

  • Delay gate

    Profile, follow list and relay list changes wait 24 hours by default; deletions wait 48 hours. You choose which kinds are held.

When something is held, the node sends an encrypted alert to your phone. You have the whole delay to say no.

Try the slipperthe real policy, running in your browser

Pick an event kind and see what the shares say. Same rules as cinderella.config.json: unknown kinds are denied, identity kinds queue for 24 hours, deletions for 48.

The veto: one key, one job

The veto key does exactly one thing: it says no. It cannot sign and holds no share. If it is stolen, the worst someone can do is block a change, never make one. That is why it can live on an old phone.

What we tested

Dry run, 2 October 2026.

  1. Changed a profile from a normal Nostr client.
  2. The share node held it and sent an encrypted alert to an Android phone.
  3. Replied “veto” from the phone.
  4. The node refused to sign; the Gateway showed it as “vetoed”.
